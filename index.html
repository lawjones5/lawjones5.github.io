Simulating the fluids that will power fusion.

Lawrence Jones

I am a PhD student in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at the University of Sheffield. My core doctoral research focuses on liquid metal fusion breeder blankets and the complex fluid dynamic phenomena associated with them—specifically magnetohydrodynamics (MHD)—using the high-fidelity Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) code CHAPsim2.

Beyond liquid metal MHD, I hold a strong interest in nuclear fusion power cycles and thermal energy extraction architectures, with a specific focus on supercritical CO2 (sCO2) Brayton cycles for high-efficiency balance-of-plant power conversion.

Research Focus

My research, in two parts

Magnetohydrodynamics is my core doctoral programme; supercritical CO2 power cycles are a long-standing secondary interest.

Magnetohydrodynamics for fusion blankets

Direct Numerical Simulation (DNS) of magnetohydrodynamic flows in conducting duct geometries — investigating Hartmann boundary layers, Lorentz force damping, turbulence anisotropy, and heat transfer under magnetic confinement.

DNS CHAPsim2 Liquid Metal Tokamak Blankets

High-efficiency sCO2 power conversion

Investigating supercritical carbon dioxide (sCO2) Brayton cycles for high-efficiency thermal power conversion — compact turbomachinery layouts and enhanced passive safety when coupled with fusion blanket heat exchangers.
